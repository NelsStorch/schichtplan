Not only local departments and troops have a shift planner. **Regions, cantons (cantonal associations), and national organizations** can also have their own – for Cevi this includes sections, member organizations, and the umbrella organization. There you can plan events that do not belong to a single troop: a cantonal camp, a national day, or a national jamboree.

In the shift planner for such a level, it is called "Organization" instead of "Troop" or "Department", and "Leadership" instead of "Troop leaders".

## Who sets up the shift planner

It can be set up by the **leadership of that level according to the association database**, for example the cantonal board or, for Jubla, the national board. Which roles qualify is explained under [Groups & roles](/en/abteilung/gruppen-und-rollen/).

<Steps>
1. Sign in with your association account. If you lead an organizational level that does not yet have a shift planner, the system will suggest creating one.
2. If you are already directed to your local troop or department, the start page there will show the notice **"You also lead…"** with <Btn>Set up shift planner</Btn>.
3. Choose a name and subdomain, and you are done. The name can be changed later, but the subdomain cannot.
</Steps>

There is exactly one shift planner per organizational level. All other leaders belonging to that level will automatically be included and receive leadership permissions upon their next login.

## Planning with a project team

Often, events are planned not by the board itself, but by a specialized project team. The project team does **not receive automatic leadership permissions** for the whole organization: otherwise they would see helpers across all events of that level. Instead:

<Steps>
1. The board creates the event (or grants specific individuals the permission **Create events**, see [Members & permissions](/en/abteilung/rechte/)).
2. They add the project team to the event's **organizing committee (OC)**: OC Lead for coordinators, OC Member for everyone else. See [Roles in the OC](/en/ok/rollen/).
3. From then on, the project team plans everything independently: shifts, access, messages, assignments. They only see the volunteers registered for this specific event.
</Steps>

Project team members only need an account (via association login or email and password), but no overarching role in the parent organization.

## Who is allowed to sign up

Just like with any department, you define sign-up permissions under [Access](/en/ok/zugang/). For higher-level events, these options are typically suitable:

- **Public** or **Association login, all departments/troops**, if the event is open to everyone.
- **Multiple departments**, if only specific troops or departments are taking part.
- **Access links**, to specifically invite individual groups, see [Access links](/en/ok/zugangs-links/).

On a higher organizational level, "Members only" means: people with an active role directly on this level (e.g. on the cantonal board or in a cantonal working group). Members of local troops underneath are not automatically included.
